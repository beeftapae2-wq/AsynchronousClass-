Section A

What is a Resume?

R

Definition

A resume is a persuasive summary of your qualifications for employment.

If you are in the job market, having a resume makes you look well organized and prepared to your prospective employers.

Why it matters

A resume is often your first impression with an employer — before any interview happens. A well-crafted resume gets you in the door; a poorly made one keeps it closed.

Section B

Types of Resume

1
Type 1

Print Resume

Printed on paper for prospective employers to scrutinize. Designed to emphasize key information using bold or italic typeface.

📄 Paper-based Bold & Italic OK
2
Type 2

Scannable Resume

Designed to be read by computers (ATS systems); formatted using a single typeface — no italics or bold to ensure clean machine parsing.

💻 ATS-friendly Plain formatting

Quick Comparison

Print Resume Scannable Resume
Read by Human eyes Computer / ATS software
Formatting Bold, italic, varied typeface Single plain typeface only
Keywords Action verbs preferred Nouns / keyword-rich

Section C · Feature 1

Organization: How to Structure Your Resume

There are different ways to organize your resume depending on your goals, experiences, and qualifications.

A

Chronological Resume

Most common format · Best for steady work history

Most Common

Lists all academic and work experience from the most recent to the oldest. Ideal if you have a consistent work history in one field.

📌 Example order: Current job → Previous job → Earlier job → Education (latest first)
B

Functional Resume

Skills-focused · Good for career changers

Skills-Based

Organized around various kinds of experience. Use this if you want to demonstrate a wide range of skills across multiple areas, or if you want to downplay specific dates.

📌 Best for: Career changers, returning workers, or those with diverse experience across fields
C

Targeted Resume

Goal-specific · Tailored to one job posting

Focused

Announces a specific goal at the top, just beneath your name. Offers only the experience and skills relevant to that particular position.

📌 Best for: Applying to a specific job posting where you want to match requirements exactly

Section D

Features of a Good Resume

2

Succinctness

Concise and parallel entries

A resume must be concise. Entries may not be written in full sentences but should be parallel in structure.

📄 Print Resume

Use action verbs: organized, designed, managed — to emphasize what you did.

💻 Scannable Resume

Use nouns as keywords: organizer, designer, manager — for ATS software to detect.

3

Comprehensiveness

Present all important details

A resume must present all important details that can gain the attention of the prospective employer.

💡 You may use the exact words from the job advertisement — provided they genuinely reflect your qualifications and experience.
4

Design

Reader-friendly and professionally packaged

The resume should be reader-friendly and professionally packaged. Design differs based on resume type:

📄 Print Resume Design

  • ✔ Appropriate spacing
  • ✔ Clear section headings
  • ✔ Uniform typeface per heading
  • ✔ Bold/italic for emphasis

💻 Scannable Resume Design

  • ✔ One standard typeface only
  • ✔ No bold or italic formatting
  • ✔ Simple, clean structure
  • ✔ Keyword-optimized content
