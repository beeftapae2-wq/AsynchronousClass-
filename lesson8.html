Section A · Concept Grounding

What is an Application Letter?

Its Purpose

Your application letter is generally the first thing prospective employers will see. It should motivate them to read your resume by providing a positive impression — showing that you have the qualifications they are looking for.

Its Role

An application letter serves as a formal introduction of yourself to your prospective employer. It accompanies your resume and must make a favourable impression so you can secure an interview — achieved through how the letter is written and presented.

Key Takeaway

Think of your application letter as your personal sales pitch. Its job is not to repeat your resume — it's to compel the employer to read your resume and invite you for an interview.

Section B · Features

Features of an Application Letter

A well-crafted application letter has three essential features that set it apart from other forms of professional writing.

1

Qualifications

Relevant · Matched · Targeted

Your letter must clearly communicate why you are interested in the position or organization, what skills you possess that match what the company is looking for, and why the employer should want to meet you.

2

Pleasing Tone

Professional · Confident · Error-free

Go beyond simply stating your accomplishments. Through your words, demonstrate the kind of employee you will be. Presentation also matters — your letter must be neat and entirely error-free.

3

Format

Structured · Business-like · Standard

Application letters follow a conventional business-like format. The most common is the Block Format, which includes the writer's address, date, recipient's name and address, salutation, body, closing, and signature.

Section C · Block Format

Structure of an Application Letter

The most widely used format for application letters is the Block Format — all text is aligned to the left margin, with no indentation. Here are its required parts:

1

Writer's Address

Your complete mailing address at the top of the letter

2

Date

The date the letter is written (e.g., April 23, 2026)

3

Recipient's Name & Address

Full name, title, company name, and address of the employer

4

Salutation

A formal greeting — e.g., "Dear Attorney Lim," or "Dear Sir/Madam,"

5

Body / Message

Your introduction, qualifications, and call to action — the heart of the letter

6

Closing

A polite sign-off — e.g., "Sincerely," or "Respectfully yours,"

7

Signature

Your handwritten signature above your printed name

Writer's Address
123 Sampaguita St., Quezon City
Metro Manila, Philippines
Date
April 23, 2026
Recipient
Attorney Maria Santos
HR Director, ABC Corporation
456 Ayala Ave., Makati City
Salutation
Dear Attorney Santos,
Body
I am writing to apply for the Junior Accountant position advertised on your company's website. With a Bachelor's degree in Accountancy from the University of the Philippines and a strong background in financial reporting, I am confident I can contribute meaningfully to your team...
Closing
Respectfully yours,
Signature
[Handwritten Signature]
Franchesca M. Morales

Block Format Tip

In block format, every part of the letter begins at the left margin — no paragraph indentations. Leave one blank line between each section for clean, professional spacing.

Section D · Writing Tips

Guidelines for Writing an Application Letter

Follow these five guidelines to write an application letter that is professional, compelling, and employer-ready.

1

Focus

Keep it relevant and professional

Professional

An application letter is not personal and should not be chatty. Keep it focused — when applying for a position, include only information relevant to that specific position. Avoid off-topic anecdotes or overly casual language.

2

Stay on Topic

State your purpose clearly from the start

Clarity

State the reason for the letter explicitly. Unlike essays or emails, letters need to announce their purpose in the first paragraph — ideally the first sentence. When applying, say so immediately.

✏️ Example opening: "I am writing for the FASIH Scholarship for students majoring in Accountancy."
3

Think of Your Letter as an Argument

Make a claim and support it with evidence

Persuasion

When asking for a job, you are making an argument. You are making a claim — that you are qualified for a certain position — and you need to support that claim with reasons and evidence such as your education, work experience, and references.

💡 Example: Franchesca Morales cites her education and work experience, and offers to supply references who will support her application.
4

Choose an Appropriate Salutation

Address your reader correctly

Etiquette

The appropriate salutation depends on how much you know about your reader:

Know name & title

"Dear Attorney Lim,"

Know name only

"Dear Jean Chavez,"

Unknown reader

"Dear Sir/Madam,"

5

Proofread

Perfection is the standard

Critical

Few writing situations demand greater perfection than professional letters — especially application letters. Typos, grammar errors, and sloppiness immediately prejudice readers against applicants. Employers will wonder: "If this applicant can't take the time to proofread, how badly do they actually want this position?"

To compete, strive for perfection. Read your letter aloud, use spell-check, and ask someone else to review it before submitting.
