Section A

Definition & Relevance

What is a SWOT Analysis?

A SWOT Analysis (or SWOT Matrix) is a strategic planning diagram that identifies an organization's key Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, and Threats.

By conducting this analysis, any organization, corporation, or company can innovate or adapt new strategies to better position itself in the corporate world.

Section B

Internal vs External Factors

Factor Type 1

Internal Factors

✅ Things we CAN control

Internal factors lie within the organization's control. They map directly to Strengths (what you do well) and Weaknesses (where you fall short).

📌 Example

Workers spending time fixing computer crashes — a solvable problem through employee training or purchasing better software.

Factor Type 2

External Factors

⚠️ Things we CANNOT control

External factors come from outside the organization. They map to Opportunities (favorable conditions) and Threats (risks from the environment).

📌 Example

Inflation rates of goods due to government regulations — an external force the company must adapt to, not control.

Section C

Benefits of SWOT Analysis

1

Identifies capabilities & resources

Reveals an organization's capabilities, resources, and competitive environment.

2

Shapes better marketing strategy

SWOT results directly inform smarter, data-driven marketing decisions.

3

Uncovers new ventures & opportunities

Surfaces unexplored opportunities that the company has not yet tapped into.

4

Helps reduce threats

Managers and directors can minimize risks by understanding current weaknesses.

5

Serves as a planning technique

Used company-wide as a strategic planning and decision-making framework.

6

Useful for individual career development

Individuals can apply SWOT to discover personal skills, opportunities, and abilities.

Section D

How to Make a SWOT Analysis

1

Define your objective

Make sure the analysis heads in the right direction by clearly defining your goal. Focusing on what matters will eventually provide the right strategy.

2

Focus on internal and external factors

Strengths

Identify the things your company does better than the competition. What advantages do you have?

Weaknesses

In what areas are competitors outclassing your company? Where do you fall short?

Opportunities

What external opportunities can help your company grow and expand into new markets?

Threats

Are there obstacles stopping the company from growing? Rules, regulations, or technology impeding growth?

3

Prioritize and rank findings

Not all factors carry equal weight. After listing them, rank each item by importance or impact so your team knows which to address first.

4

Develop action strategies

Use the completed matrix to generate strategies: leverage Strengths to pursue Opportunities, use Strengths to counter Threats, improve Weaknesses, and plan for external risks.

Section E

Sample SWOT Matrix: Coffee Shop

The following is a SWOT analysis for a coffee shop startup. Notice how internal factors (S & W) are within the company's control, while external factors (O & T) come from the market environment.

Internal · Positive
Strengths
Close to universities and public transport
Cheaper products vs. popular brands
Ambiance suited for study and leisure
Growing demand for coffee shops across markets
Internal · Negative
Weaknesses
No experience in operating a business
Lack of funds for startup capital
Less brand recognition vs. established chains
Staff needs training for operations
External · Positive
Opportunities
New products and services for expansion
Brand franchising potential
Co-branding or strategic alliances
Diverse and growing target market
External · Negative
Threats
Many well-established coffee shop competitors
Other startups entering the same space
Changing consumer tastes and preferences
Rising prices of coffee beans and supplies

Key Takeaway

A well-done SWOT lets the coffee shop leverage its location and pricing (Strengths) to capture franchise and partnership opportunities, while investing in staff training (fixing Weaknesses) to withstand market competition (Threats).
